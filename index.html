A little about me

About me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in cosmology at the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute (KASI) and the University of Science and Technology (UST) in Daejeon, South Korea.

I study the expansion history of the Universe using Type Ia supernovae, baryon acoustic oscillations, and complementary cosmological observations. My work focuses on testing the consistency of these probes and understanding what they tell us about dark energy.

Questions that drive my work

Research interests

How robust is our picture of the expanding Universe?

I combine cosmological observations and statistical inference to test the standard cosmological model, with a focus on four connected areas.

01

Dark energy &
cosmic expansion

Constraining the expansion history and investigating possible departures from the standard ΛCDM model.

02

Cosmological
consistency tests

Comparing supernova and BAO measurements using flexible, data-driven methods, including crossing statistics.

03

Type Ia
supernovae

Studying absolute-magnitude evolution and its connection to inferences about dark-energy evolution.

04

Cosmic
isotropy

Testing the angular distribution of supernovae for dipolar signatures, accounting for survey-selection effects.
